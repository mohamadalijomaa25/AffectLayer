What Mathematical Probability Does the ML Model Use?
=====================================================

Great question for your presentation! Here's the exact math behind it:

DistilBERT uses SOFTMAX PROBABILITY
-------------------------------------

After DistilBERT processes the text, the final layer of the neural network outputs
two raw numbers called LOGITS — one for NEGATIVE, one for POSITIVE.
These are just arbitrary scores like:

    logits = [-2.1,   3.8]
               ^        ^
            NEGATIVE  POSITIVE

These raw numbers are meaningless on their own, so the model applies the
SOFTMAX FUNCTION to convert them into probabilities that sum to exactly 1 (100%):

    Softmax(x_i) = e^(x_i) / SUM( e^(x_j) )

For the example above:

    e^(-2.1) = 0.122
    e^(3.8)  = 44.7

    NEGATIVE = 0.122 / (0.122 + 44.7) = 0.0027  →  0.27%
    POSITIVE = 44.7  / (0.122 + 44.7) = 0.9973  →  99.73%

So the model returns: { "label": "POSITIVE", "score": 0.9973 }

In your ml_analyzer.py (line 28), this is exactly what you capture:

    best_prediction = max(results[0], key=lambda x: x['score'])
    label = best_prediction['label']   # "POSITIVE"
    score = best_prediction['score']   # 0.9973

And then you multiply it by 100 to display it as a percentage in the UI:
    round(score * 100)  →  99%


KEY POINT FOR YOUR PRESENTATION
---------------------------------
The Softmax score is NOT the same as accuracy. A model can be 99.7% confident
it's POSITIVE and still be completely wrong — like when someone says:

    "Wow, great, another last-minute change."

sarcastically. The model sees "Wow" and "great" and outputs 99% POSITIVE confidence.
But the REAL hidden emotion is Frustration.

That is exactly the research gap your project (AffectLayer) addresses —
detecting the hidden emotion beneath a confidently wrong surface prediction.
